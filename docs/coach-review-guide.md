# Shift Happens! coach-review guide

**12U defensive-zone practice · Box + 1 · About 15 minutes**

Review whether a 12U defenseman can recognize who owns the most dangerous threat, when that responsibility changes, and how to recover if the middle opens. Use the team’s Box + 1 as the standard. Original handoff and Marked upper corner are approved baselines. Behind net and delayed exit are drafts for this review.

## Watch these reps

Open the **DefensivePractice** scene in Unity and enter **Play Mode**. In the Unity menu, use **Tools → Shift Happens → Watch Behind Net**. Start with the scripted comparisons, then use **Back to practice** for a live attempt. In practice, select **Guided** and **Variation #1** for the comparison. Use **Freer** for the final live attempt if time allows.

| Rep | Pause or watch for | Coach question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Original handoff** via **Watch First Guided Shift → Hold then close** | RD returns and gets set, then LD closes from the inside. | Can a player tell when RD actually owns net front? Is that visual cue consistent with what we teach? |
| **Hold net front** | Red crosses behind the goal, rounds the far post and comes out. RD moves toward the carrier while LD tracks Red C. | Does LD keep the right inside position throughout? Do RD’s route and Blue C’s support leave a threat uncovered? |
| **Chase the carrier** | LD follows the puck and Red looks for the slot. | Which threat did LD give up? Could a 12U player see when the lane opened and explain the recovery route? |
| **Delayed exit** | Red waits behind the net before emerging. | Does the cue still come from the play? Who owns each post and the slot during the wait? |
| **Late recovery** | LD holds through the crossing, opens the lane after Red exits, then recovers during the pass warning. | Does the review distinguish the opening, the recovery and the puck outcome? Is the slot-look threshold fair at 12U game speed? |

## Decide what the game should teach

Watch the puck and the off-puck threat at the same time. For each disputed moment, pause and note the preset, variation, approximate game time, puck location, skater and change you want. If you have desktop Review Mode open, mark the spot or lane directly on the rink and export the review.

| Decision | Record the coach’s call |
| --- | --- |
| **LD’s assignment** | When the puck goes behind the net and comes out, who does LD own? What, if anything, releases LD? |
| **RD and Blue C** | Who pressures or contains the carrier at the far post? Who owns the near-post and slot threats while RD moves? |
| **Spacing and stick** | Where should LD be relative to Red C and the net? Which passing lane should the stick take away? |
| **Scoring** | Should conceding a slot look count as Missed even if LD recovers before the pass? How much time or space should qualify as a real opening? |
| **Teaching language** | What short cue would you say on the bench? Could a player repeat it in their own words and show it on the ice? |
| **Transfer** | What should a player do if the puck reverses, Red C changes depth, or Blue wins clear possession? These are review prompts for future reps. |

## Take it onto the ice

Start with three defenders around one net. Send the puck into either corner and have one defender close, one support and one cover net front. Add a live pass option and vary the timing. Then add the wingers and play to a controlled exit. Ask the net-front defender after a rep, “Who did you have, and what made you move?” [USA Hockey D-Zone Build-Up](https://www.usahockey.com/news_article/show/703462-12u-18u-d-zone-build-up) · [USA Hockey small-area games](https://www.usahockey.com/smallareagames)

Use Chapter 10 as the systems reference and Chapter 1 to connect the recovery to a breakout. Savard’s example shows how a returning partner can communicate that they have net front. Use it to compare against the team’s own call in each preset.
